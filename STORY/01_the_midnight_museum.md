# 01 — THE MIDNIGHT MUSEUM

**Genre:** Investigation / Crime
**Tone:** Elegant, tense, deceptive — a heist film crossed with a slow-burn con reveal (think *Knives Out* meets *Ocean's Eleven*, told backward)
**Difficulty:** Medium–Hard
**Estimated playtime:** ~20 minutes
**One-line hook:** During a museum gala, the lights die for exactly 47 seconds — and when they return, the priceless Varga Blue Diamond is gone from an unbroken case, guarded the entire time by a man who never moved.

---

## PREMISE

The Kessler Museum is celebrating its centennial. The star of the collection, the **Varga Blue Diamond**, sits in a reinforced glass case at the center of the main hall, ringed by donors in evening wear. At 9:14 PM, every light in the building cuts out for 47 seconds. When they come back on, the case stands open, the diamond gone — and Marcus, the guard stationed directly in front of it, swears he never left his post and never heard the case open.

What looks like an impossible heist is actually the *cover story* for a much older, much quieter crime: the real diamond left the building six months ago. Tonight's "theft" is theater, staged by the one man everyone trusts most, to explain why the case will eventually be found holding a fake.

## CAST

**Elena Voss, 34 — Head Curator**
Meticulous, controlling, image-conscious. Inherited her late father's debt-ridden gallery and has spent years rebuilding credibility through this museum.
*Motivation:* protect her professional reputation at any cost.
*Fear:* being blamed for a breach that happened on her watch.
*Secret:* she privately had the diamond re-appraised a month ago after noticing something odd about its cut, and has been sitting on a report she's afraid to open.
*Relationships:* reports to Dominic (board chair); mentors Priya; wary of Isabelle from the start.
*Hides:* the sealed appraisal envelope, in her coat pocket all night.

**Marcus Reyes, 52 — Head of Security**
Ex-police, proud, fiercely loyal to the job — but not infallible.
*Motivation:* protect his estranged daughter, who isn't supposed to be in the building tonight.
*Fear:* being blamed for the breach on the biggest night of his career.
*Secret:* he manually disabled one hallway camera earlier in the evening so his daughter could sneak in and see him work — not to help anyone steal anything, but it's the exact blind spot the real thief needed.
*Relationships:* father to a daughter he rarely sees; respects Elena; instantly suspicious of the "caterer" he doesn't recognize.
*Hides:* why the camera was off.

**Isabelle Chen, 29 — Insurance Investigator (Meridian Underwriters)**
Sharp, methodical, quietly obsessed with pattern-matching. Has seen "perfect blackout theft" claims before — twice, both were inside jobs staged for a payout.
*Motivation:* stop a third fraudulent claim from going through on her desk.
*Fear:* being publicly wrong, or missing it again.
*Secret:* she suspected Elena within the first ten minutes, purely from financial records — and initially chases the wrong thread.
*Relationships:* professionally cold to everyone; the only person actually looking for the truth instead of managing appearances.

**Dominic Hart, 45 — Board Chairman & Major Donor**
Charming, magnetic, terrified underneath. A hidden gambling habit put him deep in debt.
*Motivation:* avoid financial and social ruin.
*Fear:* exposure.
*Secret (the actual truth of the story):* six months ago, Dominic quietly arranged for the real diamond to be swapped for a flawless replica during a routine "cleaning," and sold the genuine stone privately to cover his debts. Tonight's blackout "theft" is a decoy he hired a specialist to stage — an alibi for why the case will eventually be found holding a fake, blamed on tonight instead of six months ago.
*Relationships:* Elena's boss; publicly warm to Priya; treats Isabelle as an annoyance to be managed.

**Priya Malhotra, 24 — Night-Shift Restoration Intern**
Anxious, sharp-eyed, constantly underestimated because she's junior.
*Motivation:* be taken seriously.
*Fear:* raising an alarm and being wrong, or right and ignored.
*Secret:* for an unrelated research paper, she's been quietly logging inconsistencies in the diamond's documented provenance for weeks — without realizing she's building the exact evidence trail that unravels Dominic.
*Relationships:* intimidated by Elena; unnoticed by Dominic (his mistake); the one person nobody thinks to suspect or distrust.

## CENTRAL CONFLICT
Who stole the diamond during the 47-second blackout — and why does the physical evidence not add up?

## HIDDEN TRUTH (GM ONLY — never shown to players)
There was no theft tonight. The real Varga Blue Diamond left the building six months ago, swapped for a replica by Dominic Hart to cover his debts. Tonight's blackout was staged — a hired specialist posing as catering staff "opened" the case for show — purely to create an official police/insurance theft report that will explain the empty (or fake-filled) case *before* anyone notices it happened months earlier. Marcus's unrelated camera tampering, done for personal reasons, accidentally created the exact blind-spot window the hired specialist needed. Priya's research inadvertently built the paper trail that exposes it.

## CHRONOLOGICAL TIMELINE (real order, not reveal order)
1. ~6 months ago: Dominic arranges the swap during a "routine cleaning."
2. ~6 months ago: The real diamond is sold privately; a flawless replica goes into the case.
3. ~1 month ago: Elena notices something off about the stone, quietly gets it re-appraised.
4. Weeks ago: Priya begins independently logging provenance inconsistencies for her paper.
5. Tonight, early evening: Dominic hires a "caterer" to fake a break-in during a blackout he'll engineer.
6. Tonight, earlier: Marcus disables one camera so his daughter can sneak in.
7. Tonight, 9:14 PM: The blackout hits; the fake "theft" is staged.
8. Aftermath: investigation unravels the wrong crime and finds the real one underneath.

## FORESHADOWING → PAYOFF MAP
- E1 (envelope) → E20 (envelope opened) — the object players forget matters most.
- E5 (toolkit sighting) → E14/E16 (Dominic's real crime) — looked incriminating for the wrong reason.
- E6 (disabled camera) → E13 (Marcus's daughter) — innocent explanation for a suspicious clue.
- E8 (vanishing caterer) → E16 (staged decoy) — the "thief" was never the point.
- E10 (cleaning record) → E14/E15/E16 (the swap) — the real crime, hidden in plain paperwork.
- E7 (Elena's debts) → deliberate red herring, resolved as irrelevant by E20.

## TWISTS
- **Minor reveal (E13):** the "suspicious" hidden figure is Marcus's daughter, not a conspirator.
- **Minor reveal (E14–15):** the diamond in evidence is fake.
- **Major twist (E16):** the real diamond left six months ago — tonight's "theft" never needed to happen because there was nothing left to steal.

## RECURRING OBJECTS / MOTIFS
- **The sealed envelope** (E1 → E20): a red herring that turns out to be real, just about a different secret than expected.
- **The disabled camera feed**: appears twice, means something different each time.
- **Photographs of the diamond**: visual motif of "looking closer" that drives the whole investigation.

## WHY IT'S FUN TO PLAY
Players spend the first two-thirds of the game convinced they're solving a heist — accusing Elena, Marcus, even the wrong "caterer" — only to realize in Act 4 that the entire "theft" was a magic trick covering up a much older, quieter crime. The reward isn't catching a thief; it's realizing the thing they were investigating never actually happened the way it looked. Early drawings (the envelope, the toolkit, the disabled camera) get *re-read* with new meaning, which is the exact "wait... that mattered" moment the format is built for.
