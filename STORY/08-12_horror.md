## 08 — THE LAST PASSENGER
**Genre:** Supernatural Horror · **Tone:** Dreamlike, liminal, quietly eerie rather than violent · **Difficulty:** Medium · **Playtime:** ~20 min
**Hook:** A taxi driver picks up a strange passenger in the dead of night and drives him to an abandoned house — only to realize the passenger may not be alive. Neither, perhaps, is the driver.

**Premise:** Rafael, a taxi driver, was in a serious car accident earlier that night and is now in a coma. This entire ride is a liminal dream-journey; the "passenger" is a guide-figure helping him decide whether to wake up.

**Cast**
- **Rafael, 44** — taxi driver, currently in a coma after tonight's accident, unaware.
- **The Passenger, unnamed** — calm, patient guide-figure; not malicious, here to help Rafael choose to return to life.
- **Dispatcher voice, only on the radio** — increasingly faint and strange as the ride continues.
- **The House's Daughter, glimpsed at the end** — an echo tied to the abandoned house, representing a memory Rafael needs to release before he can wake.

**Hidden truth:** Rafael is in a coma from tonight's crash; this ride is happening entirely in his mind. The passenger is guiding him toward accepting what happened and choosing to wake up rather than staying lost in the in-between.

**Events**
1. Rafael picks up a quiet, well-dressed passenger on an empty street. *(A: picking up a quiet passenger *canon* / B: refusing a fare / C: getting into a fender-bender)*
2. The passenger gives an address that doesn't appear on any map. *(A: giving an address off the map *canon* / B: giving no address at all / C: giving an address across town)*
3. The radio crackles with the dispatcher's voice, oddly distorted. *(A: distorted dispatcher voice *canon* / B: clear normal radio chatter / C: radio silence entirely)*
4. Streetlights flicker off one by one as the taxi passes them. *(A: streetlights flickering off in sequence *canon* / B: streetlights unusually bright / C: streetlights that were never on)*
5. Rafael glances in the rearview mirror and the passenger is gone, then instantly back. *(A: passenger vanishing/reappearing in mirror *canon* / B: mirror showing an empty back seat only / C: mirror cracking)*
6. The passenger calmly asks Rafael if he remembers how the drive started tonight. *(A: calm probing question *canon* / B: passenger asks for a different destination / C: passenger falls asleep)*
7. Rafael tries to recall and gets a flash of headlights, too bright, too close. *(A: flash of oncoming headlights *canon* / B: flash of a calm morning / C: flash of an argument at home)*
8. The road stretches unnaturally long, the same streetlamp passing twice. *(A: looping, repeating road *canon* / B: road suddenly ending *B* / C: road splitting into two paths)*
9. The taxi arrives at an abandoned house that seems to be waiting for them. *(A: arriving at an abandoned house *canon* / B: arriving at a hospital instead / C: arriving back at the taxi garage)*
10. Rafael notices his own hands on the wheel look slightly transparent. *(A: translucent hands on the wheel *canon* / B: hands covered in dust *B-alt* / C: hands shaking uncontrollably)*
11. Inside the house, a child's laughter echoes from an upstairs room. *(A: unseen child's laughter *canon* / B: adult voices arguing *B-alt* / C: complete silence throughout)*
12. The passenger leads Rafael to a room with a single flickering lamp and two empty chairs. *(A: two empty chairs under a flickering lamp *canon* / B: a room full of mirrors / C: a locked door they can't open)*
13. **TWIST setup:** the passenger gently asks, "Do you know why you're really here tonight?" *(A: gentle, pointed question *canon* / B: passenger changes the subject / C: passenger asks to leave immediately)*

16. A young girl — the house's echo — appears at the top of the stairs, watching quietly. *(A: a watching child figure appears *canon* / B: a full family appears *B-alt* / C: nothing appears at all)*
17. Rafael, afraid, asks what happens if he chooses to stay in the dream. *(A: asking what staying means *canon* / B: asking to see his family instead *B-alt* / C: trying to drive away from the house)*
18. The passenger simply opens the front door and gestures toward the light outside. *(A: door opened toward the light *canon* / B: door opened onto darkness *B-alt* / C: door that won't open at all)*
19. Rafael steps toward the light, the house and passenger fading behind him. *(A: stepping toward the light *canon* / B: turning back toward the house *B-alt* / C: running back to the taxi)*
20. Ending: Rafael wakes in a hospital bed, disoriented, a nurse's voice calling his name. *(A: waking up in a hospital bed *canon* / B: waking up back in the taxi *B-alt* / C: the screen simply fades to black)*

**Twist:** There was never a supernatural passenger threatening Rafael — the whole ride was his own unconscious mind, and the "passenger" was helping him choose to live.
**Ending:** Rafael wakes up. **Alt ending:** Rafael turns back toward the house instead of the light — the story ends ambiguously on the flickering lamp, coma outcome left unresolved.
**Motifs:** the repeating streetlamp; the two empty chairs waiting to be filled; light vs. the house as recurring visual opposites.
**Why it's fun:** it plays like a classic creepy-passenger horror story right up until the final act, then quietly recontextualizes every eerie beat (the flickering lights, the looping road, the translucent hands) as symptoms of a mind fighting to wake up — trading scares for something closer to tenderness.

## 09 — THE PHOTOGRAPH
**Genre:** Psychological Horror · **Tone:** Quiet dread, grief disguised as horror · **Difficulty:** Medium · **Playtime:** ~20 min
**Hook:** An old family photograph contains a person nobody in the family remembers — and every time someone looks at it again, something about it has changed.

**Premise:** The extra figure in the photo is a family member who died young decades ago and was never spoken of again out of unprocessed grief. The photo keeps "changing" because the family keeps unconsciously trying to edit him out of memory. Once he's openly grieved, the photo settles.

**Cast**
- **Dahlia, 36** — adult sibling revisiting the childhood home after a family event, first to notice the photo.
- **Marcus, 38** — Dahlia's skeptical spouse, trying to find a rational explanation.
- **Grandmother Opal, 82** — in early memory decline, the only one who occasionally "remembers" the extra boy in fragments.
- **Uncle Theo (in the photo), deceased** — Opal's youngest son, who died young; never discussed since.

**Hidden truth:** The extra figure is Theo, a family member who died in childhood decades ago. The family collectively, silently agreed never to speak of him to spare Opal's grief — so thoroughly that even photographs of him were quietly boxed away. The photo "changing" reflects the family's subconscious discomfort finally surfacing; it settles once they finally say his name out loud.

**Events**
1. Dahlia finds an old family photo while cleaning out a closet. *(A: finding an old photo while cleaning *canon* / B: finding a locked diary *B-alt* / C: finding old home videos *C-alt*)*
2. She notices an extra child in the photo she doesn't recognize. *(A: noticing an unfamiliar child *canon* / B: noticing a blurred face *B-alt* / C: noticing a missing person *C-alt*)*
3. Marcus examines the photo, insisting it's just a family friend's kid. *(A: skeptical, rational explanation *canon* / B: agreeing something's off *B-alt* / C: dismissing it entirely, uninterested *C-alt*)*
4. Dahlia shows the photo to Opal, who touches the boy's face and grows quiet. *(A: grandmother touching the photo, going quiet *canon* / B: grandmother laughing warmly *B-alt* / C: grandmother angrily refusing to look *C-alt*)*
5. Later that night, the photo appears slightly different — the boy standing a step closer. *(A: the boy's position subtly shifted *canon* / B: the photo now in black and white *B-alt* / C: the photo now torn *C-alt*)*
6. Marcus tries to find the negative or digital copy, and finds none exist anywhere. *(A: searching for a copy that doesn't exist *canon* / B: finding a copy with the boy missing instead *B-alt* / C: finding the photo was never developed *C-alt*)*

12. Marcus finds an old newspaper clipping mentioning a young child's sudden illness in the family decades ago. *(A: an old clipping about a child's illness *canon* / B: a clipping about an accident *B-alt* / C: a clipping unrelated to the family *C-alt*)*
13. **TWIST setup:** Opal breaks down crying, finally saying Theo's full name out loud for the first time in decades. *(A: finally saying his name aloud *canon* / B: Opal denying she ever had another child *B-alt* / C: Opal accusing Dahlia of making it up *C-alt*)*
14. The family gathers around Opal as she finally tells the real story — Theo died young of illness, and grief made everyone stop speaking of him entirely. *(A: gathering to hear the real story *canon* / B: the family refusing to listen *B-alt* / C: the family already knowing, staying silent *C-alt*)*

**Twist:** No malevolent haunting — the photo's "changes" were a grief-avoidance story made visible, resolved by finally speaking a forgotten name aloud.
**Ending:** Quiet healing and remembrance. **Alt ending:** Opal's memory fades again by morning, and the family must decide whether to keep the healing they found or let it slip away too — a sadder, more realistic close about dementia and grief.
**Motifs:** the photograph itself as the central, ever-shifting object; the unopened attic box as a literal box of suppressed memory.
**Why it's fun:** it uses "the photo keeps changing" as classic horror bait, then reveals the changes were never sinister — just grief trying to be noticed, which lands as more moving than frightening by the end.

## 10 — THE EMPTY CLASSROOM
**Genre:** College Horror · **Tone:** Campus-legend dread, slow institutional guilt · **Difficulty:** Medium · **Playtime:** ~20 min
**Hook:** Students keep hearing movement and voices from a classroom that's been locked and unused for years — and digging into why uncovers an old accident someone has spent a very long time covering up.

**Premise:** Years ago, a professor's negligence with lab equipment caused a student's death, quietly buried as "an accident" with no real accountability. The haunting persists until the janitor who witnessed it finally tells the truth and the professor is held accountable.

**Cast**
- **Priya Nair, 21** — student investigator, drawn in after hearing the noises herself.
- **Old janitor Solomon, 63** — witnessed the original accident years ago, has kept quiet out of fear for his job ever since.
- **Professor Halloway, 58** — caused the original accident through negligence, has spent years quietly maintaining the cover-up.
- **The echo of Nina, 20 (deceased)** — the student who died in the accident, her presence tied to the room.

**Hidden truth:** Years ago, Professor Halloway ignored safety warnings about faulty lab equipment, and a student, Nina, died as a result during a late-night experiment. The department quietly filed it as "an unfortunate accident" with no real inquiry. Solomon witnessed it and has stayed silent out of fear. The room's activity persists until the truth is finally spoken and Halloway faces real consequences.

**Why it's fun:** it channels classic "haunted campus room" horror into something with real moral weight — the "ghost" isn't seeking revenge, just acknowledgment, which makes solving the mystery feel like an act of justice rather than survival.

## 11 — THE HOUSE THAT REMEMBERS
**Genre:** Psychological Horror · **Tone:** Slow-build dread with a hopeful, preventable-tragedy core · **Difficulty:** Medium–Hard · **Playtime:** ~20 min
**Hook:** A family moving into a new house for a fresh start starts seeing vivid replays of past events — until they realize the house isn't showing them the past. It's showing them something that hasn't happened yet.

- **Farid, 41** — father, skeptical, wants to believe it's just stress and unfamiliarity.

- **Mrs. Alvez, 70** — former resident/neighbor who lived here years ago and experienced the same visions herself, now trying to warn them.

**Hidden truth:** The visions aren't replays of the past — they're warnings of a gas leak/fire that is building toward happening very soon in the house's old, faulty piping. The family must piece the "memory" fragments together as clues and act in time to prevent it, rather than simply trying to understand old history.
